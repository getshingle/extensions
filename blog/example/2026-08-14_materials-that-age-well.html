--- title: Materials that look better in ten years summary: Some materials wear out; others wear in. A short list of the ones we come back to, and why. tag: Materials author: mia-kovac ---

A new kitchen looks good on the day it is finished. The question we ask is how it will look in ten years, after a thousand dinners and a few spilled glasses of wine.

Solid wood

Oiled oak or ash gets warmer in color and softer to the touch with use. Scratches can be sanded out and the surface oiled again in an afternoon. Veneers and lacquered boards cannot be repaired like that.

Lime plaster

Lime lets the walls breathe, which matters in older houses, and it takes small cracks and marks without looking damaged. It also gives a soft, uneven finish that paint on plasterboard never quite matches.

Natural stone

Limestone and slate develop a patina where people walk and cook. We choose honed rather than polished finishes: they show less wear and are less slippery when wet.

Linen and wool

For curtains, upholstery and rugs, natural fibers last longer, clean well and fade gracefully in the sun instead of turning brittle.

None of these are the cheapest option on day one. Over the life of a home, they usually are.
