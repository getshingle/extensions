Every renovation has the same moment: the old floor comes up, and what is underneath is not what anyone expected. Whether that moment is a nuisance or a crisis depends almost entirely on how you set up your budget.

Split the money before you spend it

We suggest clients divide their total budget like this before any design work starts:

Part Share What it pays for
Building work 70% The builder, materials and fittings
Fees and permits 15% Architect, engineer, permit and surveys
Reserve 15% Everything nobody can see yet

The reserve is not a luxury. In older buildings we use it in almost every project, for rotten joists, old wiring or a wall that turns out not to be where the drawings said.

Decide what matters most

Write down the three things you care about most, and the three you could live without. When prices come in higher than hoped, and they often do, this list makes the choices quick and calm instead of painful.

Spend on what is hard to change later

Structure, insulation, windows and wiring are expensive to redo. Taps, paint and lights are not. If the budget is tight, put the money into the first group and upgrade the second group later.

Get three prices for the same drawings

Builders can only be compared if they price the same thing. That is why we give every builder the same drawings and the same specification, and go through the prices with you line by line.
